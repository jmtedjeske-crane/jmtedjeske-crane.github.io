I.As the Decision-Maker, you review the AI reviewer

One of the ways you exercise judgment in the research process is in the role of Decision-Maker. As the Decision-Maker, you need to determine how to incorporate AI-generated feedback on a draft into your final product.

This exercise deals with prompting an AI tool for feedback on the primary law used in the document. A good critique prompt narrows the scope of what the AI looks at to make it easier to evaluate each comment that the tool makes.

Emily Pavuluri, a law librarian and the Associate Director of the Vanderbilt AI Law Lab, provides a structure for sorting critiques into five “lenses”:1

  • Accuracy
  • Structure
  • Authority (the focus of this exercise)
  • Tone
  • Strategy

Given that this is a research course, this exercise focuses on the “authority” lens, with some consideration of “accuracy” and “strategy.” We are considering two aspects of “authority”:

  • Selection: Does the memo include the best available authority?More specific questions that your prompt can ask about selection:
    • Does the memo rely on persuasive authority only when that is a strategically justified decision?
    • Is there binding authority that the memo does not cite?
    • Is there more recent authority or useful persuasive authority that the memo does not cite?
    • Is any proposition in the memo supported by too little authority?
    • Does the memo treat adverse authority appropriately?
  • Analysis: Does the memo describe and apply each item accurately?More specific questions that your prompt can ask about analysis:
    • Does each citation support the specific sentence that it follows?
    • Does the memo state any holding more broadly than the court stated it?
    • Is the memo otherwise wrong in how it characterizes any of the authority?
    • Does any conclusion claim more confidence than the cited authority supports?

You do not need to use all of these questions. Choose the questions that you determine matter most for the memo, and write them in your own words.
