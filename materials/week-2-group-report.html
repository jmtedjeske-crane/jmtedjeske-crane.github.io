Week 2: In-Class Exercise 2, Group Report

Students complete this group report in the course’s learning management system after they submit their individual reports. Each group compares the paths that its members took through the AI tool and traditional research, writes one answer for the group’s segment, and names the authority that supports the answer. Purpose

Each member of your group researched the same segment of the TechCorp and DataSolutions fact pattern, but each member chose a different tool, a different sequence of prompts, or a different point at which to switch to traditional research. In this meeting, your group compares those paths and writes one answer for your segment. When the three groups share their answers, the class assembles a full answer to the research question: whether a Pennsylvania trial court would admit the recordings as evidence.

↑ Back to top

Task

Your group does three things in this meeting. Work from the individual reports that your members submitted.

1. Compare what your members found

Answer these three questions. A sentence or two for each question is enough. I am looking for a few generalities, not a complete list of every similarity and difference.

  1. Did your members reach the same answer for your segment? If not, where did they diverge, and what explains the divergence: the tool, the prompts, or the traditional research that followed?
  2. Which path produced the best authority: a first prompt, a follow-up prompt, or traditional research in Lexis or Westlaw? Name the tool and the step.
  3. How confident is your group that you are on the right track? You had limited time and could not read the cases carefully, so answer with that caveat in mind.

2. Write one group answer

State what your group thinks the answer is for your segment, and name the authority that your group relied on.

  • Conclusion. Your group’s answer to your segment’s question, in two to four sentences.
  • Authority. The authority that best supports your conclusion. For each authority, add a sentence or short phrase that explains how the authority supports your conclusion.

You do not need Bluebook form here, and you do not need to name your members. Given the time, there is no expectation that your group resolved the question or found everything on the topic.

3. Submit and report to the class

Your group leader writes the report for the group and submits it in the Canvas text box below. Identify your group as Group 1, Group 2, or Group 3. One submission per group is enough, and the leader changes every week.

The leader then gives a verbal report to the class of about three minutes. The report covers your group’s conclusion, the authority that supports it, and how confident your group is. After all three groups report, we assemble the full answer as a class.

Time

Group discussion and submission: 10 minutes.

Discuss as a class: 10 minutes.

Criteria

This group report counts toward the In-Class Work component of your grade and toward class participation. I grade it pass/fail, and every member of the group receives the same result. A good faith effort is all that I require.
