Week 1: In-Class Group Report

Students complete this group report on a discussion board in the course’s learning management system during the first class. Before the group meets, each student builds a prompt with the Constructing the Prompt exercise, runs it in an assigned tool, and writes an individual toe-hold review. The group then compares how the tool and the framing of the legal issue influenced the answers, and the group leader posts one report for the group.

Week 1 · In-class group work

Group Report

Purpose

Your group compares two things. First, you compare the outputs that different tools produced on the same matter. Second, you compare the different ways that the members of your group framed the same legal issue.

Those two comparisons answer different questions. The first asks how the tool influenced the answer. The second asks how the framing of the legal issue influenced the answer.

Task

Your group does two things in this meeting.

Part 1. Compare your framings and your outputs

Compare the framing of the legal issue, because the other parts of your prompts depend on which tool you used.

Answer these two questions. I am looking for a few generalities rather than a detailed statement of every similarity and difference, so a sentence or two for each question is enough.

  1. How were your framings of the legal issue similar, and how were they different?
  2. Did the outputs overlap to a large degree? Was any tool an outlier that produced fundamentally different results from the rest?

Part 2. Write one group answer

Come up with what your group thinks the answer is, and name the authorities that you would pursue further. You are not resolving the matter. You are naming your next steps in the research process.

Do not worry about whether the AI is correct at this point.

Write your answer under these headings, which are the same toe-hold review headings that you will use for the final project.

  1. Conclusion. A short summary of your group’s conclusion, in one to three sentences.
  2. Statutes, Court Rules, Constitutional Provisions, and Regulations. Any potentially relevant statute, court rule, constitutional provision, or regulation that the outputs cited.
  3. Cases. No more than five potentially relevant cases. List a case only when it looks important. For each case, give three lines:
    • the case name and citation;
    • Why it matters: one or more of these reasons, separated by semicolons: Binding authority; States a legal test or definition; Closely matches the facts; or another reason that you name; and
    • Short description: a sentence or phrase that describes the case, based on what the AI told you.
  4. Secondary Sources. No more than three secondary sources that the outputs listed and that your group would review. Specify the chapter, the section, or the database if the AI provides that information.

You do not need Bluebook form here, and you do not need to name your members.

Who does what

Your assigned group leader writes the report for the group and posts it. The leader changes every week.

The leader also gives a quick verbal report to the class during the discussion. The report covers your answer to Part 1 and your conclusion from Part 2.

Post your report to this discussion board. Identify your group as Group 1, Group 2, or Group 3. One post per group is enough.

Time

Group meeting: 15 minutes.

Discuss as a class: 10 minutes.

Criteria

This group report counts toward the In-Class Work component of your grade and toward class participation. I grade it pass/fail, and every member of the group receives the same result.

A good faith effort is all that I require.
