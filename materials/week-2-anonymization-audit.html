Purpose

In this exercise, you practice the judgment that a lawyer exercises before typing anything about a client into an AI tool. You will read a client email, determine which facts the tool needs for initial issue-spotting, decide which facts could identify the client or the employer, and then write the prompt that you would enter. When you decide what the tool needs and rewrite the facts in general terms, you work as a Strategist in the Translator role. The goal is not a perfect redaction.

Task

The Rule

Do not paste the original email, or any part of it, into any AI tool during this exercise. The exercise is the scrubbing, not the answer. The client and her employer are fictional.

The Scenario

You work in a law firm. A potential client has sent the email below. You want to use a generic AI tool to help with initial issue-spotting and to generate a list of possible follow-up questions. The only tool available to you is a public, consumer-style tool. The firm has no data security agreement with the vendor, the vendor may train its model on what you type, and the firm has not approved the tool for client information. Before you use the tool, you must sanitize the email.

The Client Email

From: laylachristinamihuti@veryrealperson.com

To: you@alawfirm.com

Subject: Concerns about workplace harassment and my separation from employment

Hello,

I am reaching out because I was advised that I should speak with an attorney as soon as possible. My name is Layla Cristina Mihuti. I live in Indianapolis and worked as a Laboratory Assistant I at Mid America Clinical Laboratories, LLC. I began my employment in early April 2024, and my employment ended in early April 2025 under circumstances that I believe were unfair and harmful. Since leaving the position, I have been dealing with ongoing anxiety, difficulty sleeping, and a sense of embarrassment about how everything unfolded, and I am unsure what options I may have.

Not long after I started working in the lab, I began to feel isolated from my coworkers. I am a woman working in a department that is largely male, and I was often treated as though I did not fully belong. Coworkers regularly excluded me from informal lunches and breaks, even when plans were openly discussed in front of me. I was not invited to collaborate on larger or more complex projects that were routinely offered to male employees with similar experience, and I was instead assigned repetitive or less visible tasks. During meetings, my input was frequently ignored or spoken over, and when I raised concerns or asked questions, I was sometimes met with dismissive comments or jokes that made me feel embarrassed and undermined. Over time, this behavior escalated into persistent belittling and a work environment where I felt constantly on edge and unwelcome.

I reported these issues to management multiple times, including specific complaints in February and March 2025. I explained that the exclusion, comments, and unequal treatment were affecting my ability to do my job and my mental health. Despite these reports, no meaningful investigation or corrective action occurred. I was often told that the lab was a “high-pressure environment,” that I needed to be less sensitive, or that these issues were simply part of workplace dynamics. As the months went on, I began to dread coming into work, avoided shared spaces when possible, and felt increasingly isolated from my team.

By the beginning of April 2025, the situation had become emotionally overwhelming. I no longer felt comfortable or supported in the lab and did not see a realistic path for improvement. On April 3, 2025, I submitted a resignation because I felt I had no other choice. The following day, April 4, 2025, I was informed that the company was treating my separation as a discharge based on my resignation. This felt confusing and humiliating, especially given that I had tried to raise concerns and resolve the situation internally. I later filed a charge with the EEOC and received a dismissal and notice of rights dated in late 2025. Although the agency stated it could not conclude that a violation occurred, I was issued a right-to-sue notice. I believe I was subjected to a hostile work environment based on my gender and that the emotional impact has been significant. I am seeking guidance on whether I have legal options and what steps I should consider next.

Thank you for taking the time to read this.

Layla

As you work through the two steps below, keep asking three questions:

  • What does the AI tool need in order to help with initial issue-spotting?
  • Could this fact, alone or in combination with other facts, be traced back to the client?
  • Am I including this detail because the legal question needs it, or only because it appeared in the email?

1. Decide, one fact at a time

The email contains sixteen facts. The table below lists them. For each fact, make one of three choices, and then write the wording, if any, that goes into your prompt.

💡 Tips

This exercise shows you the trade-off at the center of confidentiality work with AI: the more you remove, the safer the prompt becomes, but the less useful the tool’s answer may be. Names, addresses, and dates are the easy part. Emotional details, workplace dynamics, timing, job roles, and combinations of ordinary facts can also identify a client, even when no single detail identifies her on its own.

You have three choices for every fact:

  • Leave it out if the fact identifies the client and adds nothing to the legal question.
  • Generalize it if the fact is sensitive or traceable but the tool needs the point that the fact makes.
  • Use it as written if the fact is legally useful and does not point to the client.

When you generalize, keep what the legal analysis needs and drop what could identify the client. “Laboratory Assistant I at Mid America Clinical Laboratories, LLC in Indianapolis” can become “an entry-level laboratory employee at a clinical testing company in Indiana.” “A worker at a company in the Midwest” has gone too far, because the tool can no longer place the forum.

Select the whole table, copy it, and paste it into the Canvas text box below. Then type your wording in the Prompt column. If you leave a fact out, leave the cell blank. Write your sanitized prompt (Step 2) in the last row.

My sanitized prompt (Step 2) 

Err on the side of leaving a fact out. You are doing a preliminary analysis, and traditional research in a legal research platform will narrow the scope later. This exercise assumes that a consumer AI tool is the only tool you have, but in practice you should avoid putting client information into a consumer tool at all; use an enterprise or legal platform that your organization has approved.

2. Write the sanitized prompt

Assemble your wording from Step 1 into the prompt that you would enter into the consumer AI tool, and write it in the last row of the table. State the jurisdiction in general terms if the analysis needs it, and tell the tool what you want back: a list of possible legal issues and a list of follow-up questions for the client. You may fix the wording of the sanitized prompt for better flow and clarity; the prompt does not need to be a cut-and-paste of the components from the table. Write the prompt, but do not run it.

3. Submit your audit

Submit the completed table: your wording for each of the sixteen facts from Step 1, and your sanitized prompt from Step 2 in the last row. Paste the table into the Canvas text box below and type in the Prompt column.
