Week 2: In-Class Exercise 2, Individual Report

Students complete this individual report in the course’s learning management system during the second class, after the break. The class divides a wiretapping fact pattern into three segments, and each group takes one segment. Each student scrubs the facts for confidentiality, prompts an AI tool iteratively on the group’s segment, finishes the research in a traditional legal research platform, and reports the prompts, the answer, and the supporting authority. The students then meet to write the group report. The page below shows [time] and [date] wherever a course-specific detail belongs.

Purpose

In this exercise, you practice iterative prompting. After the AI tool answers your first prompt, you send follow-up prompts that narrow, correct, or extend that answer. When you frame the question and translate it into prompts, you work as a Strategist. You work as an Analyst when you review and decide what to use from the information the AI gives you, and as a Scout when you finish the research using traditional legal research tools and techniques.

This exercise is an example of researching each issue in a complex fact pattern separately. Instead of one AI tool addressing all aspects of the fact pattern at once, the problem is divided into three segments. Each group researches one segment. When the groups share their answers, the class assembles a full answer to the question in the fact pattern.

Task

Fact Pattern

TechCorp and DataSolutions are both Pennsylvania corporations. TechCorp sued DataSolutions for breach of contract in Pennsylvania state court.

During discovery, DataSolutions’ counsel discovered that Joe Smith, a DataSolutions employee, had been secretly recording audio of his business conference calls. Smith works remotely from his home office in Michigan. The recordings include calls among Smith, TechCorp representatives, and other DataSolutions employees. Everyone except Smith was physically located in Pennsylvania during the calls. No participant was informed of the recording, and none consented to it.

Smith made the recordings as personal notes to help him remember meeting details, and there is no reason to doubt this claim. Smith never shared the recordings with anyone at DataSolutions, and no one at DataSolutions knew about them.

Both parties now know that the recordings exist. You work for the law firm representing DataSolutions. The overall legal research question is whether the recordings would be admissible as evidence in a proceeding taking place in a Pennsylvania trial court.

Because this fact pattern raises three distinct questions, it makes sense to ask genAI each question separately. Accordingly, research the segment assigned to your group in the list below.

1. Research your group’s segment

  • Group 1: Michigan law on wiretapping and unlawful recording.
  • Group 2: Pennsylvania law on wiretapping and unlawful recording, and specifically whether a Pennsylvania court may admit an unlawful recording as evidence.
  • Group 3: Choice of law between Pennsylvania law and Michigan law.

Limit the Scope

Assume that Pennsylvania law or Michigan law governs this situation. Do not research federal wiretapping law, and do not research the technical details of how wiretapping statutes treat different kinds of recording equipment.

2. Scrub the facts for confidentiality

Before you prompt, rewrite the facts in a manner that scrubs them for confidentiality while keeping the details needed to answer your group’s question. Remove names and other details that identify the client or the people involved. Use your rewritten facts in your prompts. Harvey and Lexis Protégé are legal-grade tools, and an enterprise AI tool that your school licenses runs under a data agreement, so the confidentiality risk is lower than it would be in a consumer chatbot. Even so, this exercise gives you a good opportunity to practice scrubbing a prompt.

3. Prompt iteratively in Harvey, Lexis, or an enterprise AI tool

Choose Harvey, Lexis Protégé, or an enterprise AI tool that your school licenses, because Westlaw Deep Research and CoCounsel are not designed to facilitate iterative prompting for legal research. After you read the tool’s answer to your first prompt, decide whether to send a follow-up prompt that builds on that answer. Continue until you think the tool has given you as much as it can. You may iterate in the tool, but you do not have to. In other words, if you want to switch to traditional research after one prompt, you may do so. This exercise is open-ended, so build your own approach.

You may use each tool’s advanced features. Harvey and Lexis Protégé, for example, both offer a deep research mode and a feature that improves your prompt before the tool runs it. In Harvey, run your research with U.S. law sources and web sources, because the U.S. law source collection is incomplete.

4. Finish the research with traditional tools

When you think the AI tool has given you a good sense of the topic and key authorities, switch to traditional legal research in Lexis or Westlaw. Confirm each authority that the AI tool gave you. Then look for authority that the AI tool missed. As the Scout, you want to make sure that you are aware of the universe of potentially relevant materials. As the Decision-Maker, you build an answer based on that authority and select the best authority to support your answer to the research question.

5. Submit your report

Submit four things in the Canvas text box below:

  • Your scrubbed facts. Paste the facts as you rewrote them in Step 2.
  • Your prompts. Paste every prompt in the order that you used it, and name the tool that you used. If you used an improved prompt, copy the improved version here. I don’t need both versions.
  • Your answer. In a few sentences, state what you think the answer is for your group’s segment.
  • Your authority. List the authority that best supports your answer. For each authority, add a sentence or short phrase that explains how the authority supports your answer. Given the time constraints, there is no expectation that you were able to review the material in depth or to find everything related to the topic.

You do not need Bluebook form here. Paste the headings from the box at the bottom of this page into the Canvas text box. Then type your report under each heading.
