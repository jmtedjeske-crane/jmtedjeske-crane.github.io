Week 1: In-Class Individual Report

Students complete this individual report as an assignment in the course’s learning management system during the first class. Each student builds a prompt with the Constructing the Prompt exercise, runs it in an assigned tool, and writes a toe-hold report that records what the output claims and which authorities it points to. The students then meet to write the group report. The page below shows [time] and [date] wherever a course-specific detail belongs.

Week 1 · In-class exercise

Individual Report

Purpose

You practice the strategist’s role in this exercise. The strategist frames the research question, translates that question into a prompt, and decides which facts can go into a given tool at all. This is very similar to traditional research, where you frame your research question and then translate it into a natural language search or a terms and connectors search. You also practice the analyst’s role, because you review what the tool gives you and then plan your next steps.

The class works in groups of four. Everyone in the group designs a prompt and then runs that prompt in an assigned tool. You will meet in your group and come up with a single answer that you will share with the class. The goal of the exercise is that you will see how the tool influenced the answer, and how the framing of the legal issue influenced the answer.

You also write your first toe-hold report. A toe-hold report records what an AI output claims and which authorities it points to, before you verify any of it. You will write these again for the capstone, so this is the low-stakes run.

🤷‍♂️ Why a toe-hold report?

The idea behind the toe-hold report is that you should not spend all of your time iterating within one AI product and verifying what that product gives you. Instead, you get a general sense of the potential legal issues, what the answer might be, and some authority to follow up on. Then you move on to more traditional research. We will discuss this concept in more depth later in the class.

Task

If you are assigned Westlaw Deep Research, start now

Run your report before you do anything else, because Deep Research takes significantly longer than the other tools. Use the Concise report, not the full report. Start the report, and then go back and work through the online exercise while it runs.

1. Build your prompt

Open the online exercise, Constructing the Prompt. Work through Section III, where you switch each of the nine elements on and watch what each one adds to the prompt and what it rules out. Then work through Section IV, where you write your own prompt for the Dating Disasters matter one element at a time.

The Dating Disasters hypothetical is what you are working with for this assignment. Build your prompt in the online exercise and copy it out, and then run it in your assigned system. You may tweak the prompt before you run it.

Legal AI vs. General AI

Westlaw Deep Research, Lexis Protégé, and Bloomberg Law run inside a legal database, so each tool supplies the jurisdiction, the source set, and the currency limits through its own filters. Your prompt carries your issue and your facts, and you set the rest with the filters. Harvey and general AI tools such as Copilot run on your prompt alone, so your prompt will be more involved.

2. Run your prompt

Run your prompt in your assigned tool and read what comes back.

Do not worry about whether the AI is correct at this point.

3. Write your toe-hold report

Begin with the tool you ran, the prompt you ran, and your own framing of the legal issue. Then write your report under these headings, which are the same toe-hold report headings that you will use for the final project.

  1. Conclusion. A short summary of the output’s conclusion, in one to three sentences.
  2. Statutes, Court Rules, Constitutional Provisions, and Regulations. Any potentially relevant statute, court rule, constitutional provision, or regulation that the output cited.
  3. Cases. No more than five potentially relevant cases. List a case only when it looks important. For each case, give three lines:
    • the case name and citation;
    • Why it matters: one or more of these reasons, separated by semicolons: Binding authority; States a legal test or definition; Closely matches the facts; or another reason that you name; and
    • Short description: a sentence or phrase that describes the case, based on what the AI told you.
  4. Secondary Sources. No more than three secondary sources that the output listed and that you would review. Specify the chapter, the section, or the database if the AI provides that information.

You do not need Bluebook form here. Copy the headings from the box at the bottom of this page, and type your report into the Canvas text box below.

4. Submit

Submit your toe-hold report here, to me. Your group submits a separate report to the discussion board, and that is a different assignment.

Time

Work on your own: 30 minutes.

Group meeting: 15 minutes.

Discuss as a class: 10 minutes.

Criteria

This exercise counts toward the In-Class Work component of your grade and toward class participation. I grade it pass/fail.

A good faith effort is all that I require.

Turn-in window You may turn in this assignment until [time] on [date].
If you miss class You still make up this work, even if your absence is excused. We will work out a deadline.
Scoring Pass = 95%. I score the In-Class Work component as a whole, not assignment by assignment. Reductions are expected to be rare, and you will usually, though not always, receive a warning first.
