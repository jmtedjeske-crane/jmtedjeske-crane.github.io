Week 7: Final Reflection or Harvey Certification

Students complete this assignment in the course’s learning management system in place of a seventh class session. Each student chooses one of two options: a reflection on short “manifestos” that law students wrote about AI, or Harvey Academy’s free Legal Engineering Certification. Week 7: Final Reflection or Harvey Certification

Key Takeaways

  • Class does not meet on [date]. This module replaces the class session, and completing it counts as your attendance for Week 7.
  • Choose one of two options: (A) a reflection on AI and legal research, or (B) Harvey Academy’s Legal Engineering Certification.
  • If you choose Option A, write 400 to 750 words.
  • If you choose Option B, either email me your Harvey badge or write a reflection of 400 to 750 words on the certification.
  • Submit your work by [time] on [date].
  • This module is graded pass/fail and counts toward your In-Class Work grade.

Purpose

This module lets you choose which of two assignments to complete, so that you can pick the work that interests you more. Option A asks you to consider other law students’ viewpoints on AI and to reflect on your own views. Option B asks you to complete Harvey’s training on how lawyers design AI workflows, and then to judge that training against what you did in this course. Harvey’s certification treats legal engineering as a job, so not all of its content will apply to you. Much of the content is still relevant, because you will likely design your own AI workflows in practice.

Time Estimate

Each option should take you about two hours.

Grading

This module is pass/fail, and it counts toward your In-Class Work grade, which is worth 15% of your course grade. I grade the module on substance and on whether you followed the instructions. A good-faith submission that follows the instructions passes. I record a submission that passes on time at 95% of its value, and I record a late submission that passes at 85% of its value.

Deadline

Submit your work by [time] on [date].

Task

  1. Choose one option. The two options are described in the next section.
  2. Complete the work. For Option A, read at least three of the listed pieces. For Option B, complete the certification.
  3. Submit your deliverable by [time] on [date]. The How to Submit section below explains where each deliverable goes.

The Two Options

Option A: Reflection on AI and Legal Research

The pieces listed below are short “manifestos” on AI. Third-year students at Vanderbilt Law School wrote six of them for a practicum on AI and the law. Read at least three of the pieces. Each piece is brief, so I recommend that you read all of them.

  • [Reflections on AI from the instructor’s previous students are not provided in this version.]
  • Human First, Machine-Assisted: A Roadmap for Tomorrow’s Lawyers
  • GenAI: Some Rules of Thumb for Law Students
  • Harnessing AI: Your Mentor, Strategic Ally, and a Tool for Responsible Experimentation
  • Navigating the Great Unknown
  • Wondering if AI Can Help? Just Try It.
  • AI Might Not Be What You Think It Is, and That’s Okay

After you read the pieces, write your own reflection of 400 to 750 words. Do not go over 750 words. In your reflection, explain where you agree and where you disagree with the students’ perspectives and advice, and then state your own position. Because this is a legal research course, connect at least part of your reflection to legal research. You may draw on topics that we covered in class or on experience that you gained outside class. You may also discuss aspects of AI beyond legal research.

I might give anonymized versions of some Option A reflections to future classes. If you do not want me to share your reflection, tell me so when you submit it.

Option B: Harvey Academy Legal Engineering Certification

Harvey offers the Legal Engineering Certification for free through Harvey Academy. The certification covers legal knowledge for AI work, AI and technology, process design, and the adoption of AI within a legal team. Harvey awards a badge when a learner completes the certification with a score of at least 80% on the final assessment.

Complete the certification, and then earn credit in one of the following two ways:

  • Email your Harvey badge. When Harvey awards you the badge, email the badge, or a screenshot that shows your name and the badge, to me at [instructor email address]. Use the subject line “Week 7 Harvey Badge.”
  • Write a reflection. Write a reflection of 400 to 750 words on the certification. Do not go over 750 words. In your reflection, explain what you learned from the certification, and compare Harvey’s account of a legal engineer’s work with the research work that you did in this course. You may choose this way even if you passed the final assessment.

Using AI in Your Reflection

You may use generative AI to help you draft a reflection, but you may not paste AI-generated text into your submission as your own work. You also do not need to use AI for a reflective assignment like this one. If you do use AI, I suggest a two-step process. First, write a rough draft yourself, preferably in complete sentences, although an outline also works. Then ask an AI tool to help you refine the draft. Your final reflection must give your own views.

How to Submit

If you write a reflection for Option A or Option B, submit it to this Canvas assignment. Type or paste your reflection into the Canvas text box. Do not upload a document. At the top of your reflection, state which option you chose.

If you choose Option B and email your Harvey badge, you do not submit anything to Canvas. I will record your credit in Canvas after I receive your email.

Criteria

Your Week 7 work passes when it meets all of the following criteria:

  • If you chose Option A, you read at least three of the listed pieces, and your reflection explains where you agree and where you disagree with the students’ views, states your own position, and connects at least part of that position to legal research.
  • If you chose Option B and emailed your Harvey badge, the badge or screenshot shows your name and the badge.
  • If you chose Option B and wrote a reflection, your reflection explains what you learned from the certification and compares the certification’s account of legal engineering with your research work in this course.
  • Each reflection runs 400 to 750 words.
  • You submitted your reflection or emailed your Harvey badge by [time] on [date]. Work that meets the other criteria but arrives late is recorded at 85%.
