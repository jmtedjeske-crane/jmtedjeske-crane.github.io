AI and Legal Research

AI and Legal Research is a one-credit, seven-week experiential course that Julie is teaching at Penn State Dickinson Law for the first time in the fall 2026 term.

Three Roles for the Human in the Legal Research Loop

Back to top

The course organizes its exercises around three roles in which the researcher exercises professional judgment. These roles apply to both traditional and AI-augmented research processes. They come from Julie’s article Teaching Legal Research Students to Be the “Human in the Loop”, which Perspectives: Teaching Legal Research and Writing published in 2026.

  • StrategistSecondary role: Translator
  • AnalystSecondary role: Scout
  • Decision-MakerSecondary role: Communicator

Goal: Teach students to have an ownership mindset and to aim for excellence, not just to avoid sanctions.

Course Learning Objectives

Back to top

At the end of the course, you will be able to:

  1. Write clear prompts by understanding your research goals, any confidentiality issues, and what each research system can do.
  2. Critically evaluate AI-generated legal analysis to check if it is accurate, complete, well-reasoned, authoritative, and supported by reliable sources.
  3. Decide how to incorporate generative AI into a legal research workflow.
  4. Explain and reflect on the impact of generative AI in legal research, legal practice, and the larger society.
  5. Build a sustainable habit of tracking developments in generative AI.

Weekly Overview and Associated Documents

Back to top

The course runs for seven weeks. Weeks 1 through 6 each meet in person for one class session, and Week 7 is an asynchronous module. Open a week to read its plan.

Week 1: How Generative AI Works, and Prompting

This session begins with a syllabus review. We will briefly explain how generative AI and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) produce answers, and you will test those explanations in a short hands-on exercise. The session also introduces the Learn, Build, Polish framework for AI uses and the Strategist, Analyst, Decision-Maker framework for AI-augmented legal research.

In the prompting exercise, each of you will build a prompt for one assigned tool by following that vendor's own prompting advice, and you will run the prompt on a research problem. Your group will then compare the tools' answers. The session ends with a written reflection on your takeaways from the class and on what you hope to learn in this course.

Materials: Slides (PowerPoint) · Constructing the prompt (interactive exercise) · Introductions discussion prompt · End-of-class reflection · Basic terms and prompting handout (Word) · Sample prompts for the start of a research project (Word)

Assignments released

  • AI Deep Dive assignment released (pass/fail)
  • Final Capstone Project released (traditional grading)
  • Weekly AI Updates discussion boards open (pass/fail)
Week 2: Confidentiality and Progressive Prompting

This week covers how the professional responsibility rules on confidentiality, including ABA Formal Opinion 512, apply to AI use. In the first exercise, you will rewrite a fact pattern as a prompt that keeps the facts that matter to the legal analysis and removes the details that identify the client.

In the second exercise, you will run five rounds of increasingly specific prompts in one tool and record how each round changes the tool's answer. Your group will then write one answer to the research question and report it to the class. The session ends with a written reflection on AI policies in school and work.

Assignments due

  • AI Deep Dive approval request due by [time] on the day of class, if your option needs approval
  • Weekly AI Update (post by [time] on the Sunday before class; 4 of 5 required for Weeks 2 to 6)
Week 3: Hallucinations and Verification

This week covers the errors that AI tools make in legal research: fabricated cases, wrong citations, inaccurate quotations, overstated holdings, persuasive authority presented as binding, and missing controlling authority.

In the exercise, your group will check a brief by hand. Your group will then run the same brief through a document analyzer and compare the errors that the software caught with the errors that you caught. The session ends with each of you writing your own verification checklist.

Assignments due

  • Weekly AI Update (post by [time] on the Sunday before class; 4 of 5 required for Weeks 2 to 6)
Week 4: AI Versus Secondary Sources

This week treats an AI-generated research report as a bespoke but unreliable and unstable secondary source. In the first exercise, you will compare an AI tool’s answer to a research question with a treatise, practice guide, or other traditional secondary source that answers the same question.

In the second exercise, you will apply Mike Caulfield's SIFT method (Stop, Investigate the source, Find better coverage, Trace claims to the original context) to a claim about law or legal technology that you choose. The session ends with a written reflection on how you use secondary sources and AI.

Assignments due

  • Weekly AI Update (post by [time] on the Sunday before class; 4 of 5 required for Weeks 2 to 6)
Week 5: Memo and Brief Critique Session

This week treats AI as a revision tool that does not replace your judgment. You will start with a short legal memo or brief, either something you wrote or a sample that I provide, and you will prompt an AI tool to critique the memo or brief. The goal is to reinforce an ownership mindset in which you decide whether to use certain AI-generated feedback while rejecting other suggestions.

The in-class reflection will include a short reading on Rebecca Fordon’s concept of using AI output as a research “toe-hold,” along with reactions to the reading.

Assignments due

  • AI Deep Dive due by [time] on the day of class (pass/fail)
  • Weekly AI Update (post by [time] on the Sunday before class; 4 of 5 required for Weeks 2 to 6)
Week 6: Statutory Research and Course Takeaways

This week focuses on using AI tools for statutory research, with an emphasis on effective dates, amendments, and the cases that interpret a statute. In the exercise, you will compare an AI tool’s answer to a statutory question with an annotated code.

We will also complete course evaluations in class.

Finally, you will combine your takeaways from the course into a list of tips for 1Ls about AI and legal research.

Assignments due

  • Weekly AI Update (post by [time] on the Sunday before class; 4 of 5 required for Weeks 2 to 6); last opportunity
Week 7: AI Certification or Reflection (asynchronous module, no class session)

Class will not meet in person. You will complete one of two options: the Harvey Academy Legal Engineering Certification or a reflection exercise. The option you complete counts toward your In-Class Work grade, and completing it counts as your attendance for this week. I will post detailed instructions.

If we miss a class earlier in the term, that week will be the asynchronous week, and this will be the “make up” week.

Assignments due

  • Certification or reflection due by [time] on the last day of classes (pass/fail; part of In-Class Work)
  • Final Capstone Project due by [time] on the last day of classes (traditional grading)
